		\noindent RISC-V is an open, global technology whose
                impact varies by region. In Europe, various
                communities, gathering both academic and industrial
                interested in RISC-V and open-source hardware began
                organizing local meetups very early on. After a while,
                it became clear that a larger, continent-wide event
                that would bring people together would be entirely
                appropriate. This article summarizes how organizers of
                some of these events eventually got together and
                joined forces with other players to create the “RISC-V
                Summit Europe” series, one of the three world-class
                RISC-V Summits.

\section{Introduction}

The emergence of the open ISA RISC-V has created numerous
opportunities in circuit design and software: (1) Opening up the field
of microarchitecture to scientific research; (2) Providing
opportunities for states and industries to make independent choices
for computing cores, tailored to their needs; (3) The emergence of new
collaboration models between research and industry; (4) The
posisbility for software developers and researchers to impact hardware
solutions.

Before this, the duopoly of Intel/AMD and ARM on processor cores made
research in microarchitecture very difficult. Their proprietary nature
prevented the implementation of these instruction sets. Even in cases
where access to the source code of commercial cores was granted, the
licensing issues made it difficult to collaborate about and experiment
with new hardware features or extensions, for both the industry and
academia. Therefore, the knowledge of ISA implementation and processor
design was very scarce in Europe, as most of teams working on CPU were
based mostly in the USA, in Cambridge (UK), in Israel, and a couple of
other locations.

As a consequnce, the potential benefits of RISC-V for Europe came with
severall challenges: (a) There were very few, if any, curiculum for
processor design at the master or PhD level; (b) Both academia and
industry had very small teams knowledgable and involved in processor
design; (c) There was neither nationals nor Europeans ecosystems to
take on RISC-V. In other words, everthing had to be constructed almost
from scratch.

This paper tries to summarizes a key point of the construction of the
Eureopan RISC-V ecosystem: how the burgeoning Eureopan RISC-V
community went from several regional, small-scale events to eventally
become a large and key contributor to the \emph{RISC-V Summit Europe},
one of the three world-class event of the global RISC-V community

The remaining of this document is organized as follows: First a
presentation of the various RISC-V events that emerged organicaly
localy; Second, details on the gathering of a several groups involved
into these locals events towards a larger, European event to unify our
ecosystem; Third an overview of the first four \emph{RISC-V Summit
Europe}; And last a conclusion with some perspectives.

\section{Towards a European event}

\subsection{European Organizers, Unite!}

During the \citetitle{WEEK-3} held in Paris on early May 2022,
discussions on how to make a European-wide event were started. And
informal agreement was reached to have the next event, whatever it
will be, to be during late Spring 2023 at BSC in Barecelona.

The discussions kept going throughout the 2022 Summer on how to get
together at the European level. Keep in mind that although organizres
of local events was very excited about RISC-V, and that we agreed that
we need to unify at the European level, we had a number of issues to
address: (1) Although there were previous collaborations within the
group, not everybody knew everybody else; (2) What kind of balance
should be maintained among the different countries? (3) What kind of
balance should we have between academia and industry?

We ended up putting together a Steering Committee of 13 people: 4 from
Austria and Germany, 4 from Spain, 4 from France and 1 from
Switzerland. For the groups with 4 seats, it was agreed that they
would pick 2 people from industry, and 2 from academia. This went
smoothly and we were able to start the SC work.

The next issues were to define the event format, to decide where the
event would be held, what would be presented, and and how to run
it. We ended-up with a strong consensus on each of these issues. The
format would be a full week as 1+3+1 days with 1 day of working group
meetings, 3 days for the main conference, and one day for \emph{side
events} like open or closed workshops related to RISC-V or open
hardware, that could go from technology demonstration up to private
consortium meetings of publicly funded projects. For the location, we
aggreed that the first three instances will be hosted by the three
large groups (Barcelona in 2023, Munich in 2024 and Paris in 2025).
