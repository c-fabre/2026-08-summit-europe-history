	\begin{abstract}
		\noindent RISC-V is an open, global technology whose
                impact varies by region. In Europe, various
                communities, gathering both academic and industrial
                interested in RISC-V and open-source hardware began
                organizing local meetups very early on. After a while,
                it became clear that a larger, continent-wide event
                that would bring people together would be entirely
                appropriate. This article summarizes how organizers of
                some of these events eventually got together and
                joined forces with other players to create the “RISC-V
                Summit Europe” series, one of the three world-class
                RISC-V Summits.

\section{Introduction}

The emergence of the open ISA RISC-V created numerous opportunities in
circuit design and software: (1) opening up the field of \textmu
architecture to scientific research; (2) providing opportunities for
states and industries to make independent choices for computing cores;
(3) enabling the emergence of new collaboration models between
research and industry; and (4) the possibility for software to impact
hardware solutions.

Before this, the duopoly of Intel/AMD and ARM on processor cores made
research in \textmu architecture very difficult. Their proprietary
nature prevented the implementation of the instructions sets. Even in
cases where access to the source code of commercial cores was granted,
the licensing issues made it difficult to collaborate about and
experiment with new hardware features or extensions. Therefore, the
knowledge of ISA implementation and processor design was very scarce
in Europe, as most of teams working on CPU were based mostly in the
USA, in Cambridge (UK), in Israel, and a couple of other locations.

As a consequence, the potential benefits of RISC-V for Europe came
with several challenges: (a) there were very few, if any, curriculum
for processor design at the master or PhD level; (b) both academia and
industry had very small teams knowledgeable in processor design; (c)
there was neither national nor European ecosystems to take on RISC-V;
and (d), due to the proprietary nature of existing ISAs, the software
community was taking existing architecture as a given, and was not
used to collaborate with hardware communities on processor design.

In other words, everything had to be constructed almost from scratch.

This paper tries to summarizes a key point of the construction of the
Eureopan RISC-V ecosystem: how the burgeoning European RISC-V
community went from several regional, small-scale events to the
\emph{RISC-V Summit Europe}, one of the three world-class event of the
global RISC-V community

The remaining of this document is organized as follows: First a
presentation of the various RISC-V events that emerged organically
locally; Second, details on the gathering of a several groups involved
into these local events towards a larger, European event to unify our
ecosystem; Third an overview of the first four \emph{RISC-V Summit
Europe}; And last a conclusion with some perspectives.

One of the main opportunities of RISC-V is to enable actual
collaboration on hardware and software research, i.e. co-design.  In
2018, one of the distinctive features of the national ecosystem was
that INRIA was one of the main institutions dedicated to software
research, with some activities in \textmu architecture, while
conversely, the CEA carried out the bulk of chip design activities,
with some work in basic software.  The French events --~see
Table\;\ref{fr}~-- started in October 2018, and expanded gradually in
scope and openness until May 2022. They have been basically co-managed
by CEA and INRIA since 2019.

The \citetitle{MTG-1} \cite{MTG-1} was set up on short notice, on
October 2018 in Grenoble, and was organised in a hurry by a committee
of CEA researchers.  Presenters and attendees were academics, and
attendance was on invitation only.  The \citetitle{WEEK-1}
\cite{WEEK-1}, in October 2019 in Paris, was a dual event. The main
event was expanded to 2 days. Its organisation committee welcome
individuals from other research institutions, like
\href{https://inria.fr}{INRIA},
\href{https://www.univ-ubs.fr/fr/index.html}{UBS}, or
\href{https://tima.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr}{INPG/TIMA}, together with
some industrials like
\href{https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/research-and-innovation}{Thales
  R\&T} and
\href{https://www.st.com/content/st_com/en.html}{STMicroelectronics}. The
presentations were from both academia and industry and still on
invitation. One of the new development was the association with a
research community, \citetitle{GDR-SOC2} \cite{GDR-SOC2} to add a
one-day \emph{side event}. Registration to the 2+1 days were open to
everyone for the first time. The \citetitle{WEEK-2} in March/April
2021, was the first step towards international cooperation. First by
welcoming European researchers into the organization committee, and
second by inviting \citetitle{OPENHW}, a then Canada-based foundation,
to organize a one day side event. Participation was open, but
presentations at the 2+1 days event were still on invitation. Then the
\citetitle{WEEK-3} \cite{WEEK-3}, in May 2022, was the first really
international event we had in France, with a proper PC, CfP, and six
exhibitors. One of the wise decisions made by the PC was to accept as
posters the submissions that were not accepted for the plenary while
still being relevant to the community.

\section{Towards a European event}

The road to the 1st European-wide event, the \citetitle{BCN-2023} was
a two-steps process. During the Summer of 2022, organizers of several
local events joined together into an informal SC. Immediatly after,
during the Fall of 2022, the SC joined forces with \citetitle{RISCV}
to put together a shared organisation to run the Barcelona summit.

\subsection{European Organizers, Unite!}

During the \citetitle{WEEK-3}, held in Paris on early May 2022,
discussions began on the possibility of organizing a Europe-wide
event. An informal agreement was quickly reached to host the next
event at BSC in Barcelona, tentatively scheduled for late spring 2023.

The discussions kept going throughout the 2022 Summer on how to get
together. Keep in mind that even though organizers of local events
were very excited about RISC-V and a Europe-wide event, we had a
couple of issues to address: (1) Although there were previous
collaborations within the group, not everybody knew everybody else;
(2) What kind of balance should be maintained among the different
countries? (3) What kind of balance should we have between academia
and industry?

We ended up quickly putting together a Steering Committee of 13
people: 4 from Austria and Germany, 4 from Spain, 4 from France and 1
from Switzerland. For the groups with 4 seats, it was agreed that they
would pick 2 people from industry, and 2 from academia. This went
smoothly and we were able to start the SC work.

The first SC decisions were to agree on an event format, how to build
its program, the team that would be in charge, and to decide where the
events would be held. We easily reached a strong consensus on each of
these issues.

Our format would be a yearly full week as 1+3+1 days. With 1 day of
tutorials and working group meetings, 3 days for the main conference,
and 1 day for \emph{side events} like workshops (open or closed)
related to RISC-V or open hardware, such as technology demonstration
or private consortium meetings of publicly funded collaborative
projects. The program would include sponsors keynotes, invited talks
and keynotes. A CfP would be issued, and submissions will be reviewed
by a PC. The best submissions would be presented in plenary sessions
while most of others would be accepted as posters.  For the location,
we agreed that the three next events will be hosted by the three main
countries: Barcelona in 2023, Munich in 2024 and Paris in 2025. As for
the team, we picked a LCC close to the SC\footnote{The first three LCC
for Barcelona, Munich and Paris were actually all academic SC
members.} and planned to team up with \citetitle{RISCV} and hire a
local PCO.
