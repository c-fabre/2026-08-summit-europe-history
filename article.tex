	\begin{abstract}
		\noindent RISC-V is an open, global technology whose
                impact varies by region. In Europe, various
                communities, gathering both academic and industrial
                interested in RISC-V and open-source hardware began
                organizing local meetups very early on. After a while,
                it became clear that a larger, continent-wide event
                that would bring people together would be entirely
                appropriate. This article summarizes how organizers of
                some of these events eventually got together and
                joined forces with other players to create the “RISC-V
                Summit Europe” series, one of the three world-class
                RISC-V Summits.

\section{Introduction}

The emergence of the open ISA RISC-V has created numerous
opportunities in circuit design and software: (1) Opening up the field
of microarchitecture to scientific research; (2) Providing
opportunities for states and industries to make independent choices
for computing cores, tailored to their needs; (3) The emergence of new
collaboration models between research and industry; (4) The
posisbility for software developers and researchers to impact hardware
solutions.

Before this, the duopoly of Intel/AMD and ARM on processor cores made
research in microarchitecture very difficult. Their proprietary nature
prevented the implementation of these instruction sets. Even in cases
where access to the source code of commercial cores was granted, the
licensing issues made it difficult to collaborate about and experiment
with new hardware features or extensions, for both the industry and
academia. Therefore, the knowledge of ISA implementation and processor
design was very scarce in Europe, as most of teams working on CPU were
based mostly in the USA, in Cambridge (UK), in Israel, and a couple of
other locations.

As a consequnce, the potential benefits of RISC-V for Europe came with
severall challenges: (a) There were very few, if any, curiculum for
processor design at the master or PhD level; (b) Both academia and
industry had very small teams knowledgable and involved in processor
design; (c) There was neither nationals nor Europeans ecosystems to
take on RISC-V. In other words, everthing had to be constructed almost
from scratch.

This paper tries to summarizes a key point of the construction of the
Eureopan RISC-V ecosystem: how the burgeoning Eureopan RISC-V
community went from several regional, small-scale events to eventally
become a large and key contributor to the \emph{RISC-V Summit Europe},
one of the three world-class event of the global RISC-V community

The remaining of this document is organized as follows: First a
presentation of the various RISC-V events that emerged organicaly
localy; Second, details on the gathering of a several groups involved
into these locals events towards a larger, European event to unify our
ecosystem; Third an overview of the first four \emph{RISC-V Summit
Europe}; And last a conclusion with some perspectives.

The \citetitle{MTG-1} \cite{MTG-1} was set up on short notice, on
October 2018 in Grenoble, and was organized by a committee of CEA
researchers.  Presenters and attendees were academics, and attendance
was on invitation only.

The \citetitle{WEEK-1} \cite{WEEK-1}, in October 2019 in Paris, was a
dual event. The main event was expanded to 2 days, and the committe
added individuals from other research institutions, like
\href{https://inria.fr}{INRIA},
\href{https://www.univ-ubs.fr/fr/index.html}{UBS}, or
\href{https://tima.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr}{INPG/TIMA}, and some
industrials like
\href{https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/research-and-innovation}{Thales
  R\&T} and
\href{https://www.st.com/content/st_com/en.html}{STMicroelectronics}. The
presentations were from both academia and industry and still on
invitation. A \emph{side event} was organized by a nation-wide
research community \cite{GDR-SOC2} on a single day. Registration to
the three days were open to everyone.

\section{Towards a European event}

The road to the 1st European-wide event, the \citetitle{BCN-2023} was
a two-steps process. During the Summer of 2022, organizers of several
local events joined together into an informal SC. Immediatly after,
during the Fall of 2022, the SC joined forces with \citetitle{RISCV}
to put together a shared organisation to run the Barcelona summit.

\subsection{European Organizers, Unite!}

During the \citetitle{WEEK-3}, held in Paris on early May 2022,
discussions began on the possibility of organizing a Europe-wide
event. An informal agreement was quikcly reached to host the next
event at BSC in Barcelona, tentatively scheduled for late spring 2023.

The discussions kept going throughout the 2022 Summer on how to get
together. Keep in mind that even though organizers of local events
were very excited about RISC-V and a Europe-wide event, we had a
couple of issues to address: (1) Although there were previous
collaborations within the group, not everybody knew everybody else;
(2) What kind of balance should be maintained among the different
countries? (3) What kind of balance should we have between academia
and industry?

We ended up quikcly putting together a Steering Committee of 13
people: 4 from Austria and Germany, 4 from Spain, 4 from France and 1
from Switzerland. For the groups with 4 seats, it was agreed that they
would pick 2 people from industry, and 2 from academia. This went
smoothly and we were able to start the SC work.

The first SC decisions were to agree on an event format, how to build
its program, the team that would be in charge, and to decide where the
events would be held. We easilly reached a strong consensus on each of
these issues.

Our format would be a yearly full week as 1+3+1 days. With 1 day of
tutorials and working group meetings, 3 days for the main conference,
and 1 day for \emph{side events} like workshops (open or closed)
related to RISC-V or open hardware, such as technology demonstration
or private consortium meetings of publicly funded collaborative
projects. The program would include sponsors keynotes, invited talks
and keynotes. A CfP would be issued, and submissions will be reviewed
by a PC. The best submissions would be presented in plenary sessions
while most of others would be accepted as posters.  For the location,
we aggreed that the three next events will be hosted by the three main
countries: Barcelona in 2023, Munich in 2024 and Paris in 2025. As for
the team, we picked a LCC close to the SC\footnote{The first three LCC
for Barcelona, Munich and Paris were actually all academic SC
members.} and planed to team up with \citetitle{RISCV} and hire a
local PCO.
